LinkedIn's Date posted filter stops at "Past 24 hours". By then a good job can already have hundreds of applicants. There is still a simple way to see only the jobs posted in the last hour, or even the last 15 minutes. One catch: it now only works when you're logged out.

Short answer: open LinkedIn Jobs in a private window (logged out), search, pick Past 24 hours in Date posted, then in the address bar change f_TPR=r86400 to f_TPR=r3600 and press Enter. The number is in seconds, so 3600 = 1 hour.

Step by step

  1. Open LinkedIn Jobs logged out

    Open a private window (Ctrl+Shift+N in Chrome, Ctrl+Shift+P in Edge), go to linkedin.com/jobs/search and search as usual, for example "data analyst" in your city. Don't sign in: logged in, LinkedIn now sends you to its AI-powered search, which ignores anything shorter than 24 hours.

  2. Choose "Past 24 hours"

    Open the Date posted filter and pick Past 24 hours. The page address now contains f_TPR=r86400 (86,400 seconds = 24 hours).

  3. Change the number in the address bar

    Click the address bar, find r86400 and replace it with r3600. Press Enter.

    https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=data%20analyst&f_TPR=r864003600&sortBy=DD

  4. Sort by "Most recent"

    Add &sortBy=DD to the address (or pick Most recent if the page offers it), so the newest job is on top.

  5. Bookmark it

    Save the page as a bookmark and open it in a private window. Reload it every so often to see new ones. When you find a job, sign in to apply.

f_TPR codes: what r86400, r3600 and r900 mean

f_TPR is LinkedIn's Date posted filter in the search address. The value is r plus a number of seconds: f_TPR=r86400 means "posted in the last 86,400 seconds", which is 24 hours. Use any number of seconds. These are the useful ones:

Very short windows like 15 minutes often show few or no jobs for narrow searches. Start with 1 hour and go shorter if there are plenty.

Good to know

  • Older jobs can still slip in. LinkedIn sometimes mixes promoted and reposted jobs into the list. Check the "posted X ago" line before you apply, or see how to remove promoted and reposted jobs.
  • Logged in, it no longer works. LinkedIn now sends signed-in users to its AI-powered search (/jobs/search-results/). There, anything shorter than 24 hours is treated as 24 hours, and there is no "Most recent" sort. We tested this in October 2026: r3600 showed jobs 2 to 21 hours old. Logged out, the same search showed only jobs under an hour old. More in how to get classic job search back.
  • Logged in, EarlyApply helps. Its time buttons hide everything older than you pick, and Job Radar checks LinkedIn's newest-first list for you and alerts you within minutes.
  • It doesn't refresh by itself. New jobs only appear when you reload the page, so you have to keep checking, or get an alert within minutes instead.
  • Not in the mobile app. The app has no address bar. Use a browser.

Why the first hour matters

Recruiters usually read applications in the order they come in, and many stop once they have a good shortlist. A job that has been up for a day can have hundreds of applicants ahead of you. Applying in the first hour puts your profile near the top, before LinkedIn shows the job to everyone else.

Questions

Can I filter LinkedIn jobs by the last hour?

Not from the menu: LinkedIn's Date posted filter only offers Past 24 hours, Past week and Past month. The search address has a setting called f_TPR: set it to r3600 for the last hour (3,600 seconds). Since LinkedIn moved logged-in users to its AI-powered search, this only works when you are logged out, for example in a private window.

What does f_TPR mean in a LinkedIn URL?

It is the Date posted filter. The value is the letter r followed by a number of seconds: r86400 is 24 hours, r3600 is 1 hour, r900 is 15 minutes.

Does this work in the LinkedIn app?

No. The app has no address bar to edit. Use LinkedIn in a browser on your computer, or open the edited link in your phone's browser.

Why do I still see older jobs?

LinkedIn sometimes mixes in promoted and reposted jobs that are older than your filter. Look at the "posted X ago" line on each job, or use a tool like EarlyApply that hides them for you.
